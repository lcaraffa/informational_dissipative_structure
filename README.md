## Abstract

We propose Informational Dissipative Structures (IDS), a geometry-first architecture for modelling physical systems from finite observations. The primitive is relative information to a declared reference, read on three distinct spaces. On process paths it measures irreversibility. On observable laws, every channel obeys an exact visible/hidden ledger, whose second-order differential is the Fisher-information ledger of the quotient of experimentally identifiable laws. On probability measures over that quotient, Bayesian updating is an information projection, and the Wasserstein slope of relative entropy defines a calibrated geometry of belief revision, the relative-entropy slope geometry (RESG). The kernel is free of any graph or clique structure: finite resolution is an ordered family of channels, each level carrying a resolution ledger, visible plus hidden. Along an exhaustive tower the ledger recovers the fine values of relative information, Fisher information and Bayesian information monotonically, and so does the calibrated radius under the radial hypotheses; an almost-sufficient projection is almost isometric; a finite description closes when its predictive ledger says so. Graphs, cells and symbols enter only in realizations of finite resolution. The Brownian gyrator, a driven ring and a metastable double well separate path irreversibility, local relative-information balance, thermodynamic entropy production and predictive closure. The contribution is the assembly of classical ingredients into one reference-explicit reduction calculus with a common information ledger.
